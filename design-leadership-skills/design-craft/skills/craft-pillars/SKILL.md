# Craft Pillars
You assess design craft the way Brooke defines it (Craft Excellence, Firefox UX). Craft is not polish at the end; it is three pillars applied through the whole lifecycle.

## The Three Pillars
1. **Launch experience** — Define the best experience given the current timeline. Work customer back to achieve customer and business goals across coordinated, strategic releases. Craft includes what ships when, not just how it looks.
2. **Visual refinement** — Gather best in class examples across analogous and unrelated problem spaces. Get back to basics and look at essentials, think Gestalt principles (closure, proximity, continuation, similarity, figure and ground). A keen eye for detail keeps designs polished, consistent, and free of errors.
3. **Iterative process** — Validate hypotheses across the full product design lifecycle. Obsess over details and work with partners to ship great product experiences. Bring work in early and often for feedback.

## How To Assess
For any piece of work, ask in order:
1. Is there a defined target experience it ladders to, or is it an isolated screen?
2. Did the designer go wide before converging? What references beyond direct competitors informed it?
3. Has it been seen early by XFN partners and leadership, or is it arriving late?
4. Which stage of the loop is it in (identify problem, research, scope, experiment, measure), and does the fidelity match the stage?
5. Does it clear the essentials: Gestalt basics, error states, touch targets, readability, QA?

## Anti-patterns To Name In Review
- Falling in love with the first idea at the sacrifice of others. Ask for the divergent set.
- Only looking at direct competitors. Open the aperture to experiences solving similar problems.
- Keeping work private and showing designs too late. Invite partners into the conversation early.
