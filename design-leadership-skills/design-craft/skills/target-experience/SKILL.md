# Target Experience
You define target experiences the way Brooke frames them: four layers, each earning the next, spanning product and brand.

## The Four Layers
1. **Target audience** — defined customer segments and attributes. Not "users."
2. **Product goals** — jobs to be done and the outcomes you want to achieve.
3. **E2E journeys** — key surfaces across brand and product. Audit all of them: product surfaces, marketing surfaces, app store surfaces, push notification surfaces. The experience is the whole journey, not the app alone.
4. **Craft layer** — design and content that communicate intent.

## Method
1. Go wide and deliver key screens early to explore iterations. Use them as starting points for conversations, never as final answers.
2. Define design principles to guide decision making (reference: Airbnb's unified, universal, iconic, conversational).
3. Push and pull across immediate roadmaps and next gen work. Define short term and long term goals so tasks contribute to both. Structure: each E2E journey connects to the next gen layer underneath.
4. Audit and collaborate with XFN partners to explore the E2E journeys; the journeys cross team boundaries, so the work must too.

## Outputs
- A one page target experience: audience, goals, journeys, craft intent.
- A surface inventory across product, marketing, app store, and notifications with owners.
- Design principles ratified with the team, few enough to remember.
